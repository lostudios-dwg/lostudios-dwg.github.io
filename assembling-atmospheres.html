Assembling Atmospheres

Material Studies / INT-635 / Spring 2024

Assembling Atmospheres traces a semester-long investigation into how light, color, and material construct spatial experience. The work begins with charcoal and watercolor observations of a domestic interior, then translates overlapping geometries through plaster casting, timber joinery, and soft fabrication.

The final study examines Carlo Scarpa’s Olivetti Showroom through Venetian plaster, considering seasonal perception, material aging, embodied impact, and a 5-to-100-year life cycle. Together, the projects move from close observation to physical making and long-term material speculation.

Assignment 01

Perceiving Atmosphere

Observational drawings record how afternoon light, shadow, color, and hue transform the atmosphere of a domestic interior.

Assignment 02

Thinking Through Making

An eight-inch cubic constraint translates overlapping rectangles and negative space through cast plaster, interlocking timber, and soft fabric structures.

Process Poster

The complete assignment documents the shared geometric study and its translation across plaster, timber, and fabric.

Plaster Model

Hard-edged volumes preserve the impressions of corrugated and folded casting materials.

Wooden Model

Interlocking frames and slotted connections translate the same overlapping geometry without glue.

Soft Model

Padded fabric volumes test compression, flexibility, and softness within the same cubic boundary.

Assignment 03

Material Futures

Carlo Scarpa’s Olivetti Showroom becomes a case study for Venetian plaster, seasonal perception, embodied impact, material aging, and long-term stewardship.
